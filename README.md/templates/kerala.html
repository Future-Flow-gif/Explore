Heritage and Historical Sites

Historical and heritage places of Kerala include ancient temples, forts, palaces, churches, and old towns that show the culture, traditions, and history of the region. These places reflect the royal life of kings, early trade, religious harmony, and traditional architecture. They are important because they tell stories of Kerala’s past, including ancient civilizations, colonial rule, and cultural development. These sites are protected to preserve Kerala’s rich heritage and to promote cultural tourism.
Mattancherry Palace : Kochi, Kerala: Mattancherry Palace is a historic palace famous for its beautiful murals and royal artifacts. It shows the cultural and royal life of old Kerala.
Bekal Fort : Kasaragod, Kerala: Bekal Fort is a large sea-side fort built for defense. It reflects Kerala’s military history and traditional fort architecture.
Padmanabhapuram Palace : Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala: Padmanabhapuram Palace is a grand wooden palace of the Travancore kings. It shows traditional Kerala architecture and royal lifestyle.
Muziris :Kodungallur, Kerala: Muziris is an ancient port city known for early trade with other countries. It shows Kerala’s rich history of commerce, culture, and religious harmony.

Tradition and Customs

Kerala has a rich tradition and strong customs influenced by its history and culture. People follow simple living and respect elders and family values. Traditional dress like Kasavu saree for women and mundu for men is commonly worn during festivals and special occasions. Festivals such as Onam and Vishu are celebrated with rituals, floral decorations, and traditional food. Respect for all religions and cultural harmony is an important custom in Kerala.
Onam: Onam is the biggest festival of Kerala, celebrated to welcome King Mahabali. It is marked by floral designs (Pookalam), boat races, traditional dances, and the Onam Sadhya feast.
Vishukkani Rituals: Vishukkani is a New Year ritual where people see auspicious items like flowers, rice, coins, and lamps first thing in the morning for good luck.
Kathakali Dance: Kathakali is a famous classical dance of Kerala known for colorful costumes, detailed makeup, and storytelling through expressions and gestures.
Theyyam: Theyyam is a traditional ritual art form where performers dress as gods or spirits. It is mainly performed in North Kerala and reflects ancient beliefs and customs.

Festivals and Events

Kerala celebrates many colorful festivals that reflect its rich culture and traditions. Onam is the most important festival, celebrated with flower designs, boat races, and the grand Onam Sadhya feast. Vishu marks the Malayalam New Year and is celebrated with the Vishukkani ritual. Thrissur Pooram is a famous temple festival known for decorated elephants and fireworks. Attukal Pongala is a major women’s festival, while Christmas and Eid are also celebrated, showing religious harmony in Kerala.
Onam: Onam is the most important festival of Kerala, celebrated to welcome King Mahabali. It includes Pookalam, boat races, traditional dances, and the Onam Sadhya.
Vishu: Vishu marks the Malayalam New Year and is celebrated with the Vishukkani ritual. People believe seeing auspicious items brings good luck for the year.
Thrissur Pooram: Thrissur Pooram is a famous temple festival known for decorated elephants, traditional music, and grand fireworks.
Attukal Pongala: Attukal Pongala is a unique women’s festival where millions of women offer Pongala to the Attukal Amma temple.

Folkdance and Music

Kerala has many traditional dances and music forms. Kathakali is a dance-drama with colorful costumes. Mohiniyattam is a graceful dance performed by women. Thiruvathirakali is done in groups during festivals. Theyyam is a ritual dance representing gods. Folk songs like vanchipattu accompany dances and celebrations.
Kathakali: Kathakali is a classical dance-drama with colorful costumes and expressive gestures that tell stories from epics.
Mohiniyattam: Mohiniyattam is a graceful dance performed by women, known for slow, elegant movements.
Koodiyattam: Koodiyattam is an ancient Sanskrit theatre tradition performed in temples, combining acting, music, and gestures.
Theyyam: Theyyam is a ritual dance where performers dress as gods or spirits, mainly in North Kerala, reflecting traditional beliefs.

Traditional Food and Cuisine

Kerala is famous for spicy and flavorful food. Sadhya, served on banana leaves, is a traditional feast with rice, curries, and payasam. Popular dishes include appam with stew, puttu with kadala curry, and seafood like fish and prawn curry. Kerala cuisine reflects local ingredients and traditional cooking.
Sadya: Sadya is a traditional feast served on a banana leaf, including rice, vegetables, curries, and payasam, usually during festivals like Onam.
Appam and Stew: Appam is a soft, lacy rice pancake served with a mild vegetable or meat stew.
Puttu and Kadala Curry: Puttu is steamed rice flour with coconut, served with spicy black chickpea curry called kadala curry.
Karimeen Pollichathu: Karimeen Pollichathu is a popular Kerala fish dish, where pearl spot fish is marinated, wrapped in banana leaf, and grilled.

Handcrafts and Cultural Art Forms

Kerala is known for traditional handcrafts and arts. Coir products, Aranmula mirrors, and wood carvings are popular. Mural paintings decorate temples, and Kathakali masks and costumes are made for performances. These crafts reflect Kerala’s rich culture.
Kasavu Saree: Kasavu saree is a traditional Kerala saree with golden borders, worn during festivals and special occasions.
Aranmula Mirror: Aranmula mirror is a unique handmade metal mirror, famous for its craftsmanship.
Coconut Shell Handcraft: Coconut shells are used to make decorative items, utensils, and souvenirs.
Wood Carving and Kathakali Mask: Wood carvings decorate temples and homes, while Kathakali masks are made for classical dance performances.
